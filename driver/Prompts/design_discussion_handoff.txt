Forecast type and action history · 1.5–1.6, 3.8, 10.5
  Before   Forecast verbs mean guidance. Predictions of outside decisions are skipped.
  After    A company’s measure outlook is guidance. Its own plan is an announced
           action. Management’s “We expect FDA approval” is an action forecast. An outsider’s
           estimate is an expectation. Retracting a prediction leaves the actual
           stage alone. A condition such as “if approved” proves no separate prediction.

Company confirmation and surprises · 4.1, 4.9, 4.14, 9.2
  Before   Only confirmed guidance is saved.
  After    One story reporting and confirming the same forecast gives one confirmed
           fact. Separate publications keep their own claims, times and confirmation.
           Only confirmed guidance qualifies as management’s outlook or as guidance
           for a surprise. Each surprise needs its same-event supporting fact for the
           same result or forecast, including any distinguishing conditions.

Outside forecasts · 2.19, 3.2–3.5, 5.9, 9.11
  Before   The fifth type has no complete format or history.
  After    Use _expectation for a measure or event. A bank keeps its history across
           signers. Keep personal forecasts, different banks and consensus distinct.
           Use forecaster in the existing fact scope and optional forecast_date for
           the decision date. A price target can lack a deadline. A revenue total
           needs a period. Keep sourced revisions and alternatives, even at equal
           amounts. An explicit reaffirmation or withdrawal needs no prior saved amount.

Company guidance withdrawal · 4.18–4.21, 7.5
  Before   Write a withdrawal on every affected forecast, including late arrivals.
  After    Save one ordinary action per source occurrence. A planned withdrawal
           needs evidence it took effect. Read effective withdrawals, exceptions
           and genuine replacements before choosing current guidance. Leave unclear
           coverage unresolved. A covered unconfirmed report stays unconfirmed in
           history. If a withdrawal ended the prior forecast, a new bare
           forecast has unknown movement. A recap does not restart a withdrawal.

Evidence within one source · 1.17, 1.24, 5.2–5.5, A1
  Before   Filling blanks or skipping the text copy can lose detail.
  After    “$10.0M, up 5%” plus a verified $10,040,000 for the same filing figure
           gives one $10.04M fact with 5% growth and both citations. An eligible
           tag may refine a saved level only with its original ID, location, old
           amount and quote, precise filed entry and approved filing-line link.
           If the existing save cannot keep that proof, keep the saved fact.

Words and numbers · 4.7–4.8, 10.4
  Before   Text values and conditions are guidance-only. A dash is undecided.
  After    Save exact words adding to a numberless metric and exact conditions
           on actions. A dash means zero only with explicit proof for that cell.

Filing evidence and waiting · 1.17, 3.9, 8.14–8.15, 9.4
  Before   The item number is informational. Some failures wait without a trigger.
  After    AI selects prepared passages or cells. Code verifies the original citation.
           Cite the exhibit when it supplies the figure. Keep item numbers on the filing.
           Wait only for a rule-permitted change. Skip and log pieces that still fit
           several claims after required checks. After checks, a filing with no proven
           unique owner or permitted retry stays on file with a recorded extraction skip.

  After    Keep an otherwise supported fact with unknown publication time. A historical

ISSUE 1 — WHO MADE THE FORECAST, AND DID THE COMPANY CONFIRM IT?

Status: OWNER-APPROVED semantic distinction.

Original problem

The target includes outside forecasts (expectation) and unconfirmed reports of
company guidance. The older rules define company_confirmed but reserve false,
while outside-forecast fields remain drafts. Merely allowing false is not enough:
current fact identity and history rules do not consistently distinguish these
claims, and different forecasters can otherwise be combined or read as revisions
to the company's own forecast.

Relevant rules: 1.22, 1.26, 3.2-3.3, the field/per-type tables, 4.4, 4.9, 5.2-5.5,
7.1, 7.5, 9.2 and 9.11. New naming/family handling also involves 1.18, 2.19-2.26
and 2.32. The current type/field tables do not fully specify expectation.

What the owner agreed to

The owner initially summarized the idea as company_confirmed being true when a
forecast comes from management and false otherwise, with expectation separate.
The assistant corrected the phrase "false otherwise". The owner then said:
  "Understood. I agree with that rule."

The agreed rule is:

  A. The company or its management stated or clearly confirmed its forecast:
     fact_type = guidance; company_confirmed = true.

  B. An outside source reports an alleged company forecast without company
     confirmation, and the attribution is sufficiently clear:
     unconfirmed guidance; company_confirmed = false.

  C. An analyst makes their own forecast, or a source reports consensus:
     fact_type = expectation; company_confirmed is not applicable.
     Do not populate it with false merely because management did not speak.

  D. It is unclear who made the alleged company forecast:
     skip and record the reason. Unclear authorship is not automatically false.

The field concerns evidence of company authorship/confirmation. It is not a
judgment that the prediction will come true. It is not a model-confidence field.

A news article directly quoting management can support company_confirmed=true.
The publication channel and the speaker are different things. A publisher
reporting another person's forecast is not automatically the forecaster.
An analyst question/assumption, management's polite acknowledgment or silence
does not establish confirmation (1.26). Keep needed Q&A context and actual
speaker evidence, rather than trusting stored question/answer labels alone.

The last explanation also stated that saving and history must respect the
distinction, so unconfirmed guidance cannot replace confirmed guidance. Record
that protection with the agreed semantic rule; do not implement only the boolean.

Examples and reason

All examples concern ACME's 2027 revenue in US dollars:
  - ACME management: "We expect revenue of $10 billion."
    This is company-confirmed guidance.
  - Research Firm A: "We expect ACME revenue of $12 billion."
    This is Firm A's expectation, not an ACME guidance increase.
  - A newspaper: "Sources say ACME management expects $11 billion."
    This reports an unconfirmed company forecast, not necessarily the newspaper's
    own estimate. Keep the reported attribution and lack of company confirmation.
  - A newspaper directly quotes ACME management making the $10 billion forecast.
    The newspaper channel does not turn the management statement into an outside
    forecast or make it unconfirmed solely because it is reported in news.

If Monday's $10 billion company forecast and Tuesday's $12 billion analyst
forecast are read as one history, the system can falsely report a $2 billion
company guidance increase. That can corrupt comparisons, daily explanations,
learning and predictions. Equal numbers from two forecasters are still two
claims, not proof that the forecasts are one fact.

ISSUE 3 - WHEN DOES A TABLE DASH MEAN ZERO?

Status: OWNER-APPROVED. The approval and exact agreed wording appear below.

Problem and current behavior:
  Rule 10.4 explicitly leaves this undecided: earlier tests interpreted a prior-
  year dash as zero, but there is no approved rule permitting that interpretation.
  A printed zero is already a real value (3.48), not missing data. A dash is not
  automatically a printed zero. The current discovery prompt therefore retains
  dashes unresolved; G3 in find_facts_review.json records this open choice.

Recommendation in plain words:
  Read a dash as zero only when the same source explicitly establishes that
  meaning for that cell. Keep the original dash and the evidence establishing
  its meaning. If the source gives another meaning, keep that meaning; if unclear,
  do not extract a number from the dash and record the reason.

Illustrative cases, not a keyword list or executed tests:
  - The applicable table note says dashes represent zero: a cell containing a dash
    can supply zero, with the note and cell preserved as evidence.
  - The applicable note says dashes mean not applicable: no numeric value follows.
  - The source never establishes the dash's meaning: retain the dash unresolved,
    not zero. Other supported values in the row/source still proceed.
  - A note describes a nonzero reporting threshold or a bound: preserve what was
    actually stated under the ordinary amount/bound rules; do not turn it into
    an exact zero or invent the unknown amount.

Why this is the smallest safe rule:
  It reuses the normal reader, source evidence, numeric fields and issue reporting.
  It requires no separate dash detector, model call, field, maintained symbol
  dictionary or filing-specific exception. A numeric default could fabricate a
  prior-year result and affect changes, comparisons and conclusions downstream.
  A conservative unresolved value is visible; an invented zero looks like data.

Evidence and scope boundaries:
  - A source explanation must actually apply to this cell/row/table and period;
    a nearby legend for a different table is not proof. The source may establish
    zero in its applicable prose as well as a legend. Conflicting applicable
    explanations stay unresolved. Common accounting habits or a model's belief
    that a dash "usually" means zero do not establish this case.
  - Do not calculate a missing value from totals, use a different filing to repair
    the current one, or use a separately tagged zero to manufacture the visible
    text fact. Existing source-only and tagged-data route restrictions remain.
  - The quote remains verbatim: never replace the dash with a fabricated printed
    0. Retain the cell's original location and the governing explanation under
    existing context/quote rules. If required evidence cannot be retained, follow
    the ordinary evidence refusal rule; do not weaken it for this case.
  - A minus sign on a number, a range separator, a blank cell and a standalone
    dash are not interchangeable. Meaning is judged from the source, not a global
    string replacement. No new hardcoded list of glyphs or company conventions.
  - An unresolved dash does not become an accepted numberless metric merely to
    force a fact through. Independently supported qualitative claims may still
    qualify. An uncertain quantity is not rescued by an unknown unit (3.54).
  - With a supported current value and ambiguous prior dash, retain the current
    value and record the prior numeric gap. Do not invent the prior zero, derive
    growth from it, or drop the valid current fact. Two independently proven
    period values still produce two candidates under the owner's earlier ruling.
  - Source-proven zero remains an ordinary number: no false empty-value handling,
    arbitrary precision, automatic surprise or unstated stored calculation. Unit,
    period, measurement, subject and identity checks continue to apply.

Required rule clarification for the eventual updating bot:
  1. Resolve 10.4 with the evidence rule above and keep 3.48's printed-zero rule.
  2. Reconcile 1.13/1.17's "every stored number must appear, as printed" wording:

     the original dash and its explanation in the quote. A digit 0 need not have
     appeared in the cell, but its zero meaning must be stated by the source.

  3. Keep 1.24, 3.29/3.54, 6.11-6.12 and 8.1/8.4/8.14-8.15 consistent. No unsupported
     indefinite hold: the reader reports the uncertain value; the caller applies

  5. Before implementation, inspect the selected extraction package's pinned

ISSUE 5 - WHEN SHOULD AN ITEM WAIT FOR ANOTHER ATTEMPT?

Status: OWNER-APPROVED. The final approval and exact presented rule appear below.

The problem:
  Some clauses say to hold an unclear item without identifying anything specific
  that could make it resolvable. Examples include an ungrounded "results beat"
  (4.15) and a claim about a consolidation artifact itself (2.14). But 8.14 defines
  held as waiting for a specific trigger, and 8.15 forbids indefinite waiting for
  more evidence, time passing, guessed future filings, or another answer to an
  unchanged meaning decision. These instructions need consistent wording.

Approved principle, using the existing rule rather than new machinery:
  "Hold an item only for a specific, checkable change allowed by the rules that
   could resolve its blocker. Otherwise record the final outcome and reason.
   A later source is processed as its own event."

Plain examples:
  - The original source is temporarily unavailable: hold and use the permitted
    source-access retry. Do not claim that the evidence was searched or absent.
  - Required full-source reading/rereading is complete, but "results beat" still
    does not identify what beat what: skip that unsupported claim with its reason,
    not an indefinite hold. Keep independently supported facts in the source.
  - An unchanged identity proposal received a valid refusal: do not keep asking
    the same or another model for approval. Keep distinct meanings separate or
    skip when no permitted representation exists, under the existing rules.
  - An item actually violates a rule: reject with the reason. A possible future
    change does not turn a violation into a hold (8.14).

Why this is the smaller consistent design:
  It uses the existing outcomes, reason reporting and retry rules; no new state,
  queue, service, review process or model call is proposed. A held item has a real
  path forward; a final skip is explicit and counted. This avoids wasted repeated
  reads without treating temporary access failures as proof that no fact exists.
  It does not remove supported facts or let uncertain meaning pass validation.

Important boundaries to preserve:
  - 8.15 already allows only source unavailability as general automatic retry.
    Other holds require their rule-defined, specific change. A reader must not
    invent "a later filing may clarify it" as a trigger for vague evidence.
  - 8.12 separately permits bounded retries of technical failures or malformed/
    incomplete AI replies, with a limit fixed beforehand and attempts recorded.
    A valid no/unsure is not a technical failure. This decision preserves those
    retries and required same-source rereads; it does not mean "never retry."
  - Final not-found requires complete, error-free source coverage (8.14). Incomplete
    searches and failed/invalidated writes are not converted to final skips just
    to avoid retry work. Preserve their existing safety rule and identify the
    exact permitted repair/change needed; do not invent success or partial writes.
  - Specific existing dependency holds remain possible: e.g. the independent
    duplicate check required by 2.34, or an admitted first fact under 2.37. Later
    evidence can satisfy a separately allowed dependency without rewriting the
    earlier source's meaning. Every old fact still needs its own source proof.
  - Final means final for that decision on that evidence. Preserve the narrow
    allowed reopenings in 8.14-8.15; do not impose a forever ban on later genuine
    evidence or promise a fresh attempt merely because time passed.
  - Later sources are new source events. Reopening an older job requires the
    rule-proven connection; rerun the whole assigned job with full original
    context, not one item stripped of context. Do not borrow later evidence to
    fill earlier quotes, amounts, identity, periods or other unsupported meaning.
  - Discovery reports candidates and reasons; the caller determines saved/held/
    skipped/rejected outcomes. The prompt does not schedule retries or decide that
    storage succeeded. Preserve this boundary and per-item accounting.
  - This discussion does not approve changing the five outcomes or resolving the
    separately parked P6 status-simplification question. Do not build a parallel
    outcome model or collapse meaningful cases incidentally.

  4. Update the discovery prompt and G5 coverage only where the wording affects
