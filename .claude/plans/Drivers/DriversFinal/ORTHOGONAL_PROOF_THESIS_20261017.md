## 0. Summary

The current closeout proves a fixed sample of 173,875 micro-checks ("duties") against readings made earlier by humans and models; 86.07% is proven. The recorded blockers of the residual are unproved relations and frames, unknown individual causes, unproved governing context, and numbering conventions the readings never stated; none is a verdict on the extractor, and none can be settled from the readings alone. This thesis proposes a different oracle and a different unit of proof: the browser's own rendering of the page, compared mechanically with the extractor's record, one whole document at a time, over the whole population. The comparison is a conjunction of equalities and relation-equivalences (§3). The theorems of §4 show that they imply the structural faithfulness properties the owner requires; that the method fails closed, meaning a defect on one side can only cause a false alarm, never a false pass, unless both sides make the identical error; and which sample sizes support which statements. Most ingredients exist (§5). What is missing is described, not written: a small browser emitter, a second render of a page rebuilt from the record, a splice render for provenance, a comparator, a checker-power test and a blind audit of agreed items. §6 pre-registers a pilot with kill criteria. §7 states what the method cannot prove; the largest gap is meaning in tables whose headers are ordinary cells marked only by looks, which is most SEC tables and exactly where the prediction value sits. §9 records the panel's five designs, their critics' verdicts, and the changes they forced. §10 is the note to Root.

The honest one-line verdict: this method can certify, cheaply and for every document, that the record agrees with the browser on content, reading order, table geometry and source position; it cannot certify "faithful to a careful human" without a sampled blind audit (small, random, with an exact bound), and it cannot certify header meaning where the markup states none, which is most SEC tables.

## 1. The goal, restated from first principles

Let d be a document (a byte string) and E(d) the extractor's record of it. "Faithful" means: a careful reader of the rendered page and a reader of E(d) see the same content, in the same reading order, with the same table structure, and every piece of E(d) points back to the bytes it came from. The owner requires at least 99% in each of four areas (content, order, relationships, provenance) and no material defect, across the population of documents, with evidence a skeptical reviewer can re-check from saved artifacts. The structural fields the grader checks today (value, the text of cells above in the same column, the text of cells left in the same row, spans) are functions of table geometry and text and are covered by §4; which of those cells is the governing header, and what a number denotes, is meaning and is out of scope (§7.1).

The question is therefore not "how do we review faster" but "what is the cheapest judge of what the page shows that is more authoritative than a reading made last month". The answer is the rendering engine, which is already part of the pipeline.

## 2. Objects and definitions

2.1 Lane. HTML documents. XML has its own invariant (§7.6).

2.2 The oracle B(d). A headless-browser render of d, offline, scripts off, pinned build and window, with every stylesheet, font and picture the page names answered from saved evidence; when any is not answered, the geometry is treated as unproved: `measure` raises, or records the unanswered references when the caller passes a `refused` list, and the document is excluded (class X3, §3.7) rather than measured [VERIFIED: screen_grid.py `measure` :705-720, :718-720]. The oracle's rules are frozen and hashed before any population run; rule changes are generic, logged, and followed by a full rerun on documents not used for tuning [PANEL]. From the render we take:
- C_B(d): the set of native HTML table cells (elements of type HTMLTableCellElement) that carry a mark. The marking step inserts the comment `<!--g:n-->` immediately after every `<td>`/`<th>` start tag the scanner found, where n indexes the scanner's list of cell byte spans [VERIFIED: screen_grid.py `tag_cells` :67-87, the comment edit at :80, the cell's own end tag at :79]. The browser reads back, per marked cell: n, its table (document order), its rendered row order (rows of a table sorted by the top of their box, equal tops in DOM order, so a `<tfoot>` written first but drawn last is ordered last) [VERIFIED: JS :188-190], its box left and width, and its rowspan bounded by its row group as the table model ends it [VERIFIED: JS :192-195]. A mark whose parent is not a native table cell, or a second mark inside one cell, names nothing [VERIFIED: JS :192].
- To be added by the oracle emitter (§5, not written): per marked cell, its visible text, tag name, colspan as parsed, position among its row's cells, and box top/bottom; per table, the total count of native cells in the browser's tree (marked or not) [PANEL: a code-free two-way count]. Visible text is the concatenation, in tree order, of the cell's text nodes whose computed `visibility` is `visible`, whose ancestors have nonzero `opacity`, and which have a layout box; the existing JS applies exactly these tests to gap text [VERIFIED: JS :199-202]. `innerText` is not used.
- Two further renders per document, described in §3: the rebuilt-record render (I2c) and the splice render (I6).
- The table-model position of a cell is not exposed by any DOM API [PANEL: three critics state it explicitly, two imply it by proposing the rebuilt render instead of a second grid routine]. v0.3 therefore compares relations, not position numbers (§3, I2), and lets the browser's own table algorithm judge the record's grid through the rebuilt render.
- V_B(d): the sequence of visible text tokens: flow text in tree order, tables as their cells in rendered row order and, within a row, in DOM order (the table algorithm assigns strictly increasing columns to a row's cells in DOM order, so DOM order is column order), hidden text excluded by the computed-style tests above.

2.3 The record E(d). Units produced by `to_units` [VERIFIED: edgartools_html.py]: a table unit holds cells {r, c, rs, cs, text, header, source}. (r, c, rs, cs) are computed by `table_unit` from the tool's parsed rows as an implementation of the table model: a column occupied by an earlier rowspan is skipped (:146), spans read from the attributes (:147), a rowspan is bounded by its row group (:142, :148), the occupancy is carried per column (:151) and the cursor advances by the colspan (:152) [VERIFIED :140-152]. `source` = {byte_start, byte_end_exclusive} of the cell's own source tag, written only when the code the extractor injected into that tag (`data-prepare-cell`, :25; `codes` :99-108; `named` :111-120) survives the tool and occurs exactly once, otherwise None (both on :217) [VERIFIED :213-217]. A table that stands in a cell records its holder cell (:161, :219-220). Two facts about the tool's input matter for text comparison: the tool rewrites text before parsing (runs of spaces, a space after a point before a capital, double-escaped ampersands, zero-width characters) [VERIFIED :102-103], and the extractor removes from the tool's input only text proved hidden, the scanner's own hidden spans when its reading is certain, otherwise only the browser-proved ones [VERIFIED :119]. Circularity caveat: a later screen step may overwrite (r, c, rs, cs) with the browser's measured layout grid and mark the cell `grid: 'screen'` [VERIFIED: screen_grid.py `apply` :131-184, :166-167]; a saved cell so marked carries browser-derived geometry, and comparing it with the browser proves nothing about the extractor [PANEL: "circular agreement"]. All geometry comparisons below use `table_unit`'s values before that overwrite.

2.4 Normalization N. One function applied to both sides' text: the whitespace collapsing the rendering applies, character-reference decoding, and NBSP as the rendering treats it. N never alters digits, minus signs, dashes, parentheses, percent or currency symbols: those flip a number's meaning [PANEL]. [DECISION] The tool's own rewrites (:102-103) are not whitespace collapsing. Either N also applies these documented rewrites to the oracle side, recorded and tested rule by rule, or every occurrence counts as a violation of class "tool rewrite". The experiment (§6) counts both ways. A mistake in N can only create a mismatch, never a false pass (T7).

2.5 Truth τ. For each cell, the value a careful reader of the rendered page would record. τ is not observable directly; B approximates it as the renderer the reader looks at; in this design the blind audit (§3.9) is the only measurement of the distance between B and τ.

2.6 Three parsers. The scanner (anchor.py `Visible`, a hand-written reader of the bytes) places the marks and injects the codes; the tool (EdgarTools over lxml's HTML parser) produces E(d); the browser (Chromium's HTML parsing algorithm) produces B(d). lxml's HTML parser is not an implementation of the HTML Standard's parsing algorithm; on malformed markup the three trees can differ. Every such difference surfaces as a failure of I1, I3 or I4 or as an exclusion, never as a pass (T7); it is counted as violation class "parser divergence". In the files read, the extractor takes no table geometry from the browser except through the screen overwrite of §2.3 [VERIFIED: `table_unit` :140-152 consumes the tool's rows; `apply` :131-184 is the browser-derived write of geometry; the screen step also writes style and join facts (:876) that this thesis does not compare], which answers the panel's first-hour question.

## 3. The invariant I(d)

I(d) is the conjunction of the checks below over the non-excluded cells of d.

- I1 Bijection by provenance. φ(e) = the marked browser cell whose scanner cell start (the span the mark's n names) equals e.source.byte_start; this is the same key `apply` uses [VERIFIED :151-152]. φ is total on extractor cells with source ≠ None, injective, and onto the marked native cells of the tables E(d) represents.
- I1' Code-free counts and injection neutrality [PANEL]. Per table, the browser's count of native cells equals the count of marks it attached and equals the extractor's cell count; and the browser's visible text and cell count are identical for the original bytes and the code-injected bytes the tool received. The first catches cells the scanner never saw; the second catches a parse changed by the injection itself.
- I2a-DOM Parsed-structure equality. For every e: the browser's row index within the table, position within the row, rowspan and colspan as parsed, and tag name equal the inputs `table_unit` consumed for e. Two independent parsers of the same bytes. [PENDING] the tool's `is_header` rule must be read before header-tag equality is scored; and in most SEC tables header cells are ordinary `td` cells marked only by looks [PANEL, all five critics], so tag equality says little about header meaning.
- I2b Numbering-free relations from the record versus the original render. For matched cells a, b of one table define from E's (r, c, rs, cs): same-row(a, b), column-cover(a, b) (a's column interval meets b's), left-of(a, b), above(a, b). Define the same relations from the original render's boxes: same-row by equal box tops within tolerance, column-cover by horizontal overlap, left-of and above by edge order. Require equality of the relation sets on tables where every cell has a box (no hidden or zero-width column). This replaces the comparison of position numbers, so no numbering convention (0- or 1-based, which slot a spanning cell takes, whether hidden cells count) is needed [PANEL]; it is also the layout differential `apply` already computes in part (its return n counts measured cells where the tool's grid and the layout grid differ [VERIFIED :166]; legitimate differences at hidden or zero-width columns [VERIFIED :136]).
- I2c Rebuilt-record render [PANEL, two critics independently]. Build a plain page from E(d)'s table units: one `<table>` per unit, each cell a `<td>` with its rowspan/colspan from (rs, cs), cells placed by (r, c), text as recorded. Render it in the same browser and derive the same relations from its boxes. Require relation equality with the original render for matched cells. The browser's own table algorithm now judges whether E's grid means what the page's grid means; no second implementation of the Standard is written, and most measuring bugs cancel because the same measuring code runs on both pages. Hidden-column tables are excluded from I2b/I2c and counted (X6).
- I3 Text equality per cell. N(text(e)) = N(visibleText(φ(e))).
- I4 Sequence equality. N(V_E(d)) = N(V_B(d)), with V_E in unit order, table rows in rendered order, cells in DOM order within a row.
- I5 Decoration equality. struck(e) = struck_B(φ(e)), where struck_B is evaluated by walking each visible text node's ancestor chain under the propagation rules of CSS text decoration (a decoration propagates to inline descendants and not into inline-block, floated or absolutely positioned descendants); computed `text-decoration-line` on the text node's parent alone is insufficient [PANEL]. Evaluated only where the scanner's reading of strikes is certain [VERIFIED: anchor.py `struck_certain` :567, `plain_certain` :569]; otherwise excluded (X2). The walk is a hand-written rule and therefore shares authorship with the extractor's rule; its independent check is a pixel audit on a sample (§3.9).
- I6 Provenance by splice [PANEL, replacing v0.2's I6]. For one nesting level at a time, replace the bytes of every recorded interval [byte_start, byte_end_exclusive) of that level's cells with a one-cell sentinel carrying a unique token, render the spliced bytes, and require: the cell count is unchanged, every other cell's visible text is unchanged, and each spliced cell shows exactly its token. The browser judges the interval; no tokenizer and no second scanner is trusted. Additionally the raw bytes of the interval, decoded and normalized, must equal N(text(e)) (interval-text equality), which defeats a consistent shift that the bijection alone would accept [PANEL]. I6 also implies e.source lies inside φ(e)'s element span [VERIFIED: spans at :79].

3.7 Exclusion classes, always counted, never passed: X1 cells with source None (:217); X2 cells whose strike reading is uncertain (:567, :569); X3 documents whose offline render is unproved (:718-720); X4 marks the browser does not attach to a native table cell, or a second mark in one cell (:192); X5 XML documents a parser rejects (§7.6); X6 tables with hidden or zero-width columns for I2b/I2c; X7 render time-outs or memory failures (a failure, never a pass). The result for a document is: I holds on all non-excluded cells, with every |Xk| reported.

3.8 Checker power [PANEL]. Zero violations from a blind comparator look like success. Three tests, in decreasing strength: (a) program mutants: break one generic rule of the extractor at a time (span carry, row-group bound, hidden-text removal, code read-back) and require every mutant to produce violations; (b) replay of real past defects from the fix history (old extractor outputs against the same oracle) and require detection; (c) record-side planted faults (digit, sign, swapped cells, shifted column, flipped strike, moved interval) as a sanity check only, since the comparator catches them nearly by construction. Zero misses in n planted items bounds the miss rate below 1 − α^(1/n) (T5b), for the planted classes only.

3.9 Blind audit of agreed items [PANEL, all five critics]. The census proves agreement with the browser, not with a careful human. A reviewer reads a simple random sample of certified cells per area, stratified by difficulty (nesting depth, spans, struck or hidden text, numeric cells), as rendered crops plus the record, and reports disagreements. Zero disagreements in n items bound the false-certification rate below 1 − α^(1/n) (T8). Report by stratum; the worst stratum is the headline; numeric cells get their own gate [PANEL]. This audit is reading, the old method's instrument, but on a random sample of known size with an exact bound, which is where sampling theory applies; it is the only measurement of τ versus B in this design.

## 4. Theorems [PROVEN]

T1 (content). If I1, I3 and I4 hold for d, every visible text token of the rendered page appears in E(d) exactly once with equal normalized text, and E(d) contains no token absent from the page. Proof: I4 is equality of two finite sequences; equal sequences have equal length and equal elements at every index, hence equal multisets and equal order; I1 and I3 refine the statement to each table cell. ∎

T2 (relationships). (i) If I1 and I2a-DOM hold, every relation between cells that is a function of the parsed structure (same row, position in the row, rowspan, colspan, tag) is the same in E(d) as in the browser's DOM. (ii) If I1, I2b and I2c hold on a table, the geometric relations same-row, column-cover, left-of and above between any two of its cells are the same in E(d), in the original render and in the render of the rebuilt record. Proof: (i) a relation that is a function of equal tuples takes equal values; φ is a bijection so every pair is reached. (ii) I2b and I2c are equalities of the relation sets themselves, and relation sets equal to a common third are equal to each other. ∎
Remark. (ii) is the content of the current method's "column position" and "occupied span" duties, stated without any position number: no numbering convention exists to be unstated. Header meaning is not in (ii): which of the cells above a value is its header is not a geometric relation.

T3 (order). If I4 holds with V_B in rendered row order, the reading order of flow text and of table rows is preserved by E(d); within a row, DOM order is the logical reading order in either writing direction. Proof: as T1; rendered row order comes from box positions (:188-190), not byte order. ∎

T4 (detection and fail-closed). Call an extractor cell wrong when its value differs from τ, and the oracle wrong likewise. P(pass and extractor wrong) = P(extractor wrong and oracle identically wrong) = p_e · q, with p_e = P(extractor wrong) and q = P(oracle identically wrong | extractor wrong). The fraction of extractor errors detected is 1 − q. Proof: a pass is equality of the two values; if E's value ≠ τ and equals B's value then B's value ≠ τ and equals E's; the product is the definition of conditional probability. ∎
Remark. q is the common-mode probability; it is argued small by independence of parser, layout engine, language and authorship, except where both sides run a rule written by us (the strike walk, N, the comparator) [HYPOTHESIS]. The blind audit (§3.9) is the measurement of q's effect; T8 gives its bound.

T5 (statistics). (a) Evaluating I on all M documents gives exact counts of I-violations and exclusions; no inference. (b) Zero violations in a simple random sample of n documents drawn without replacement from M: for true rate p = D/M the probability is C(M−D, n)/C(M, n) ≤ (1 − p)^n, so the one-sided upper confidence bound at level 1 − α is at most p* = 1 − α^(1/n), conservative for a finite population. At α = 0.05: n = 59 gives 4.95%; n = 299 gives 0.997%; n = 2,995 gives 0.100% (rule of three, 3/n, to first order). (c) With v > 0 violations the binomial upper bound is the (1 − α) quantile of Beta(v + 1, n − v). (d) Cells within one document are not independent; the document is the sampling unit, cell-level rates are ratios with a document-cluster bootstrap interval, and the document-level zero-violation bound is the primary claim. (e) Each area gets its own count and bound. Proof of (b): each factor (M−D−i)/(M−i) ≤ 1 − p, so the product ≤ (1 − p)^n, decreasing in p; {p : (1 − p)^n ≥ α} = [0, 1 − α^(1/n)]. The numbers are 1 − exp(ln α / n), recomputed by the build script. ∎

T6 (exclusions reduce coverage, never add passes). By §3.7 an excluded cell contributes to no invariant and is counted; every claim is conditional on the non-excluded set with the excluded sizes stated. ∎

T7 (fail-closed under one-sided defects). A defect in N, the emitter, the marking, a parser, the splice, the rebuild or the extractor that changes a value on one side only produces a mismatch, never a pass. Proof: a pass requires equality; changing one of two equal values breaks it. ∎

T8 (what the census plus audit establishes) [PANEL-forced]. Let c = the certified share of cells (I holds), and let f be the (1 − α) upper bound on P(unfaithful | certified) from a blind audit with zero disagreements in n certified cells, f = 1 − α^(1/n) by T5(b). Then with confidence 1 − α, P(faithful and certified) ≥ c · (1 − f). Proof: P(faithful ∧ certified) = P(certified) · P(faithful | certified) = c · (1 − P(unfaithful | certified)) ≥ c · (1 − f). ∎ Consequence: the census alone bounds nothing about τ; the audit's n sets the bound (n = 299 per stratum gives f < 1%), and the headline must be the worst stratum.

P1 (orthogonality). I(d) is a function of d, B(d), E(d), N and the rebuilt and spliced renders only; no term refers to any reading K, its conventions, uncertainty flags or prerequisites. Therefore I's truth value is unchanged by any change to K, and the residual classes of the current method that are properties of K cannot occur as results of I. Proof: inspection of §2-§3. ∎ The new method has a residual of its own (X1-X7, the violation classes, and everything §7 lists); it is measured, not assumed smaller.

Cost model (statement, not promise). Current method: cost grows with duties times reviewer time per duty. This method: cost grows with documents times (three renders plus a comparison) plus human triage that grows with the number of distinct disagreement causes, plus the fixed blind audit. The panel's estimates: 1 to 10 CPU-seconds for ordinary filings, 20 to 120 seconds and 1 to 3 GB of memory for the largest annual reports; 50 to 300 disagreement causes with a long tail; a harness of 1,000 to 3,000 lines; 2 to 6 weeks to a defensible frozen verdict. All [HYPOTHESIS] until measured.

## 6. Pre-registered pilot [HYPOTHESIS until run]

Day one, three comparisons only [PANEL]: visible text (I3/I4, both N readings), injected-code read-back and counts (I1, I1'), relations from boxes (I2b). Input: 40 documents drawn at random (recorded seed) from the HTML population, plus every real past defect recoverable from the fix history as a recall test [PANEL]; no selection by old unresolved counts, no calibration against the old proven checks except convention-free ones (text, order) [PANEL]. Procedure per document: mark, render offline with the extended JS, compute `table_unit` values before the screen overwrite, evaluate, record per invariant and per cause class (extractor defect, parser divergence, oracle defect, tool rewrite, normalization), exclusions per class, wall time and peak memory; save the browser output and the record as artifacts; freeze and hash the oracle rules before the first document.
Days two and three: I2c rebuild render, I6 splice render, I5 strike walk with a pixel check on 30 cells, checker power (§3.8), and the first blind audit (§3.9) of 60 certified cells to size the full audit.
Hypotheses. H1: at least 99% of marked browser cells are matched by code (I1) and counts agree (I1'). H2: I2b violations fall into a small number of generic classes. H3: I4 holds on every document not render-refused, or fails only on normalization or tool-rewrite classes. H4: wall time per ordinary document is on the order of seconds to tens of seconds. H5: parser divergence affects a small minority of documents. H6: every replayed real defect is detected.
Kill criteria. Match rate below 95% on five or more of the 40 documents; render refused on most documents; I4 mismatches dominated by normalization disagreements that cannot be specified generically; parser divergence on a majority of documents; any replayed real defect missed; or disagreement causes that do not cluster (more than one new cause per two documents after the first 20). Any of these stops the method as-is. No score claim is made from the pilot.

## 7. What this method cannot prove, and what it needs decided

7.1 Meaning. Which cell is the governing header, which period or line item a number belongs to, scale ("in thousands"), footnote links. In most SEC tables header cells are ordinary `td` cells marked by looks, so the browser's DOM carries no header role at all [PANEL]; geometry (T2) gives "the cells above in this column" and "the cells left in this row", not which of them governs. This is the dimension where the current method is stuck and where prediction value lives. Partial extension [PANEL, HYPOTHESIS]: in inline-XBRL filings the filer's own tags carry value, period, unit and scale for tagged numbers and are an independent witness of column meaning for those numbers; not available for most press-release exhibits, and filer tags can themselves be wrong.
7.2 Pictures: identity (hash, URL, position) only, by the owner's mandate; no content.
7.3 A page whose source is wrong: both sides agree on the wrong page.
7.4 Identical two-sided errors: q (T4), measured only through the blind audit (T8).
7.5 Excluded classes X1-X7: counted, not proven.
7.6 The XML lane has its own invariant J, with no rendering. The extractor's XML route [VERIFIED: `driver/prepare/convert/xml_fields.py`, pin in §5] parses with the standard library's strict expat parser (:1), refuses anything outside the document (anchor.py `xml_parser` :249-255, external entities refused at :254), emits one field unit per text-carrying element and the whole element tree, given only for a complete parse; a document that does not parse is reported FAILED (:14-15). ## 8. Why this is orthogonal, in one paragraph

Different oracle (the rendering engine instead of past readings), different unit (a document instead of a duty), different coverage (the population instead of a fixed sample), different failure modes (render refusal, parser divergence, normalization and hidden-column tables instead of conventions and uncertainty flags), and, by P1, formal independence from the readings whose properties make up part of the current residual. The current method stays valid for what it has proven. This method adds evidence of a different kind at a different cost, with a residual of its own, and it still needs one small piece of the old instrument, reading, in the form of a random blind audit with an exact bound.
