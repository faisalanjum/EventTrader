They do not require all inference to use only one short quote. Provenance and verified fiscal metadata remain legitimate inputs; limited prior guidance use under 3.7/3.35/4.18 is not banned. Request inspection adds an important limit: these unit trials put all target evidence blocks in one shared state and told each question to use its named block. That is instruction-based evidence scoping, not hard isolation to one record's quote; `isolated` questions still share the state. Do not report these timings as a measured one-request-per-record design. The final grouping/layout must be tested as deployed, including cross-record distractors.

## 2. Build evidence and preserve the continuous quote

**Partly accept.** Give field questions the actually selected source sentences, with their order, attribution and target identity; preserve the original continuous quote and exact locations separately. ## 4. Remove metric naming from Decider

**Accept deferring canonical Driver naming/matching; reject dropping measure identification from this extraction goal.** Rule 1.14 requires blind source proposals before catalog matching. Rules 2.40–2.43 do not say the source reader may omit the proposed meaning; they govern independent identity checks afterward.

“Does this evidence say what the amount measures?” is a useful check, but a yes does not supply which quantity the record represents. A quote with revenue, EPS and a ratio can contain all three. ## 7. Whole sentence as worded value; hold several

Use the sentence freely as evidence; that is different from assigning its whole content to one value. The Paychex low-end sentence contains three distinct forecasts. Confidence: high in these constraints; worded extraction is still an open failure class after the bounded attempts.
